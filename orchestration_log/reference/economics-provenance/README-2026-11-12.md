## Not determined

- Limit weights for any model but Opus 5.5 and for 1-hour intake. Two readings of the limit around one model's work settle each; the tool's `calibrate` does the division.
- How far benchmark capability stands for capability on a team's own tasks. A count of attempts right at first check, by variant and kind of task, settles it.
- Whether a retry succeeds as often as the work-unit relation assumes. The sentence on "the capability its failed attempts leave" is reasoned, not measured.
- What Artificial Analysis counts in its dollars a task (caching in its harness is unknown). On that run an attempt's dollars rise with effort about as its output does, and output is 25 to 49 percent of them; whether Claude Code agents behave alike is unmeasured.
- Noise: one run for each variant; a gap of 0.04 in capability is inside it. `pairs.json` carries 18 other suites of the same run; on the three the author tabulated (FrontierCode-AA 2.0, Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1, the composite index) a strong model at low effort again matches a weaker one at max for a third of the dollars or fewer.
- Speeds are the vendor's API speeds. Estafette's own agents read lower (sonnet 109, opus 90, fable 76 tokens a second; unchecked).
- Tool time lost when agents share a machine; generation's share of wall time by kind of agent (measured, unchecked, kept out of the skill).
- Two harness observations: an agent launched at effort low recorded medium, and the 1-hour TTL setting for subagents was seen in force without being verified for every launch kind.

## Corrections made on the way

- "Effort moves output alone" was the author's draft and is wrong on the published run; the skill now says effort raises intake and carry through the context the added output grows.
- Wrong claims relayed from one research report before the check: SWE-bench dollars are per solved task; a stronger model at lower effort is not cheaper at every shared score; token counts by effort are published.
- The 1-hour cache held for the main session alone; subagents ran at 5 minutes until a setting changed it.

## Where the skill meets the upstream skills

These are observations; the owner ruled that reconciling the development cycle "is not our concern" in estafette, which leaves it with you.

- `agentic-delegation` says "A continued agent via `SendMessage` costs only the delta message." That holds inside the TTL and leaves out the carry of the continued context at every step.
- `agentic-delegation` assigns three tiers by work type, and `dev-cycle` a tier by risk, with "never assign a unit to haiku." The skill chooses a variant, model and effort together, by the cost of a work unit in the binding resource.
- `dev-cycle` continues the same implementer and the same reviewer after a fix. The skill counts a continuation against a fresh agent started from files.
- `agentic-delegation` treats the orchestrator's cost of an agent as "the dispatch plus a 3-sentence notification." Each notification is a step that carries the orchestrator's whole context.
- `check-back` times a wake by the pending run alone. A wake past the orchestrator's own TTL is a cold start.
