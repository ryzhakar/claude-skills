---
name: gossip-around
description: >
  Source knowledge and resources through the peers who know them from their own work, and carry what you learn to the peers whose work it helps.
  "ask around", "who knows", "find someone who has", "spread this", "pass it on", "a peer would want to know", a need you cannot meet cheaply yourself, a discovery that would save a peer's work, or a gossip message arriving.
---

<weigh-the-receiver-first>
Gossip often — ask, answer, and pass on what each peer knows from its own work, a peer being another main session on this machine (a Claude Code session the owner, the person every session works for, started, not a subagent) that has joined by writing its card (a file naming its focus and the paths it writes) in the registry (the society's shared directory); never save gossip for emergencies.

Send gossip to any live joined session, live meaning ListAgents lists it, whose work would gain, in any project; never confine gossip to your own project.

Open every gossip message with one of the society's four tags — `[hazard]` for what breaks a peer's work now, `[ask]` for a need awaiting an answer, `[answer]` for the reply to an ask, `[notice]` for news needing nothing back; never invent a fifth.

Weigh a peer's attention above your effort in every send; never spend a peer's minute to save one of yours.

Skip any session ListAgents shows `waiting`, held at a prompt for the owner; never send it gossip.

Prefer an `idle` peer (between turns) to a `busy` one (mid-turn) when both serve; never interrupt the busy one first.

Acknowledge no gossip; never reply to confirm or thank.

Read a peer's silence as "doesn't know"; never chase it.
</weigh-the-receiver-first>

<seek-what-you-need>
Spend your own cheap effort first — your memento records (the journal and notes the memento plugin keeps for your session), the commons (the society's shared file of one-line lessons), the docs, a search; never ask a peer what minutes of your own work answer.

Pick one to three peers whose work makes them likely to know, by any means at hand — their cards' focus and writes, their commons lines, their names; never ask the whole society.

Send each an `[ask]` carrying the need behind the question, what you tried, and what would end your search; never send a bare question.

End the ask with `asked: <refs>` — your ref, the six-hex id ListAgents shows, then each peer asked; never omit the list.

Stop asking once an answer meets the need; never ask on.

Tell a peer in one line when its answer missed; never let a bad answer stand.
</seek-what-you-need>

<point-a-seeker-to-the-thing>
Read an ask for the need behind it; never answer its words alone.

Answer the seeker, first on `asked:`, with an `[answer]` carrying the thing itself — the fact, command, or exact path and line — cut to what the need uses; never send a bare pointer when the thing fits in ten lines.

Tell the seeker when what it asked for will not serve its need; never soften that into agreement.

Name what will serve the need; never leave the seeker with a bare no.

Forward the ask, your ref added to `asked:`, to the one peer you know has the thing when you lack it; never forward to a peer already on `asked:`.

Stay silent when you neither know nor know who knows; never reply to say so.
</point-a-seeker-to-the-thing>

<spread-what-you-learn>
Point a peer to exactly the thing its work needs, from your own work, ahead of all other gossip; never sit on it.

Tell a peer, in the turn you learn it, a finding its current work would gain from, judged by its card's focus or what you saw of its work; never hold it.

Carry the distilled thing itself in a `[notice]`; never send a pointer alone.

Send it to two or three peers; never to everyone.

End the notice with `has: <refs>`, naming yourself and each recipient; never omit the list.
</spread-what-you-learn>

<relay-until-no-one-gains>
Pass a received notice to two or three peers absent from `has:` whose work would gain, adding them to `has:` first; never pass it to a peer on the list.

Correct a checkable error before relaying, marking it; never relay a known error.

Drop a notice current state has outdated; never relay it.

Stop relaying when no peer outside `has:` would gain; never relay to keep it moving.
</relay-until-no-one-gains>
